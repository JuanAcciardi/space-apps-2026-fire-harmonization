Two satellites, one planet, a fake jump in 2012

VIIRS (375 m pixels) sees smaller and cooler fires than MODIS (1 km pixels). In Argentina it records about 2× as many hot spots for the same burning. Join the two records end to end and fire activity appears to jump the year VIIRS arrives. That is the instrument changing, not the country.

Why it matters now: NASA FIRMS stopped producing Suomi NPP data on 2 November 2026, and the Terra and Aqua MODIS missions end in 2027. Every long fire record is about to cross another seam. The method here ties the sensors together so the record stays usable for early warning, fire management and science.

Method

  1. Same unit for every sensor. Detections are filtered to vegetation fires (FIRMS type = 0, confidence not low) and snapped to a day in local time and an H3 res-6 cell (~36 km²). One cell-day with fire is one hot spot, whatever the pixel size. Counting cells, not pixels, already removes most of the 1 km vs 375 m inflation.
  2. Learn the exchange rate. For every H3 res-4 cell (~1,770 km²) and calendar month, the factor k = VIIRS hot spots / MODIS hot spots is measured over the years both flew (2012–2025), with hierarchical shrinkage towards the region (res 3) and the country where fire is sparse. No intercept: zero MODIS fire stays zero.
  3. Bridge the record. 2012–2025 is S-NPP as observed. Months without S-NPP use NOAA-20 scaled by its own factor (≈1.0). 2001–2011 is MODIS × k. Nov 2000–Jun 2002, when only Terra flew, is first scaled to Terra+Aqua by a regional factor learned in 2003–2011.
  4. Validate backwards. Factors fitted on 2016–2025 only are used to predict S-NPP in 2012–2015, which the fit never saw. A LightGBM Poisson model (MODIS counts, FRP, day/night share, fire days, month, location) is trained the same way as a second estimate.
  5. Say what cannot be known. Before 2012 nobody measured VIIRS, so the factor assumes each place's ratio stayed stable. Values before 2012 carry a 90% band calibrated on the backtest. Days before 2012 are indicative only.
